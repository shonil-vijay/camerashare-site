Common questions

How do I get started?
With two iPhones: on the phone that will take the picture, tap Camera. On the other, tap Remote and then tap the camera's name. Choose Photo, Portrait or Video on the remote, frame the shot and tap the shutter. With one iPhone, just tap Camera and shoot.

Can I use CameraShare without a remote?
Yes. Tap Camera and it works as a full camera on its own: Photo, Live, Portrait and Video, lenses and zoom, exposure, timer, flash, grids and Assist. The chip at the top says "Waiting for a remote". If someone opens CameraShare on another iPhone and taps Remote, they can join at any time, and then both phones control the same camera.

What is Assist?
Optional help to frame the shot. It looks at the live preview and shows up to two hints at a time, such as where to place your subject, headroom, someone cut off at the edge, how to level the camera, a backlit or over- or underexposed shot, closed eyes or someone looking away, or a tip like trying Portrait or 0.5x. It can also show face and subject boxes, a level line, grids you can combine (rule of thirds, 4×4, golden ratio, golden spiral, golden triangles, harmonic armature, diagonals, centre cross, square), a histogram, clipping zebras and a bar with ISO, shutter speed, EV, white balance and frame rate. On the remote, that bar also shows the camera phone's battery and a heat warning; free storage and recording time left are shown only on the camera phone. Everything is worked out on your phones; nothing is sent to us or anyone else.

How do I turn on Assist?
Tap the sparkles button in the top bar of the camera screen, on the remote or on the camera phone. It turns yellow when Assist is on (it's off by default). To choose which hints and overlays you want, tap the chevron in the top bar and then the sparkles button in the strip, or open Settings (the gear on the home screen) → Assist. Each phone has its own Assist settings.

Why did a hint appear?
Assist spotted something worth fixing in the current frame, like a tilted horizon, a face near the edge or a bright window behind your subject. Many hints come with a fix: tap the hint to apply it (for example, an exposure change, a different lens or mode, or focus on the subject). If you don't need it, tap its x or swipe it away. You can turn off any kind of hint in Settings → Assist.

The remote doesn't find the camera.
Keep both phones close together, with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth turned on in Control Center (you don't need to be connected to a Wi-Fi network). Make sure Local Network access is allowed for CameraShare on both phones in Settings → Privacy & Security → Local Network. Keep the camera phone's screen open on the Camera view.

Do I need internet or Wi-Fi?
No. The phones connect to each other directly, so it works in a park or on a mountain. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth only need to be switched on.

Where are my photos? How do I save them to Photos?
The camera phone saves every photo and video to your Photos library automatically, and also keeps it in CameraShare's Album. The first time you take a shot, it asks to add to Photos; if you say no, your shots are still in the Album. To stop the automatic saving, open Settings (the gear on the home screen) and turn off "Save to Photos automatically" under "When this phone is the camera".
The remote keeps its copies (smaller photos, and a 720p copy of each video) in its own Album, and doesn't save to Photos unless you turn that on in its Settings. To save from either Album, open it from the home screen or the thumbnail next to the shutter, tap Select, choose your shots and tap Save to Photos. Live Photos and videos are saved as Live Photos and videos. You can also share or delete them there. Copies on the remote are regular still photos, so the camera phone's Photos library is the place for Live Photos and Portrait shots.

Will I lose my photos if I delete the app?
The Album is stored inside CameraShare, so deleting the app deletes it. Anything already saved to Photos (including what the camera phone saved automatically) stays in Photos, so save anything else you want to keep first. The Album is included in your normal iPhone backups.

Which lenses can I use?
Whatever the camera phone has: 0.5x, 1x, 2x or 3x on the back, plus the front camera. The remote only shows the lenses that phone supports.

Why does it ask for the microphone?
To record sound with videos and Live Photos. Only the camera phone asks, and only when you switch to Video or turn on Live Photos. If you say no, videos are recorded without sound.

Portrait isn't available.
Portrait needs depth from the camera phone: an iPhone with two or more rear cameras, or the front TrueDepth camera. If the camera phone has neither, Portrait doesn't appear as a mode on the remote. In Portrait, the remote only shows the lenses that support it, and switches lens for you if needed.

A video didn't arrive on the remote.
The remote gets its copy after recording stops, so long videos take a little while. Keep the phones connected and close together until it arrives. Check that "Send videos to the remote" is on in the camera phone's Settings (the gear on the home screen). If the phones disconnected, the video is still in the camera phone's Album at full quality.

I said no to camera, microphone or Photos access.
Tap Open Settings in the app, or go to Settings → CameraShare (Settings → Apps → CameraShare on iOS 18 and later), and turn the permission back on.

Does CameraShare cost anything?
No. It's free, with no ads, in-app purchases or accounts.
